# Product principles

These are decision rules for Sample Room OS, not marketing slogans. They help contributors judge whether a proposed feature improves real work.

## Frontline workers are users, not data-entry endpoints

People on the shop floor are primary users. A feature must respect their time, attention, devices, and working conditions; it cannot treat them only as a source of management data.

## Human input should be the last resort

Capture information from the work already being done, established records, scanning, or sensible defaults before asking a person to type it again. Required manual input needs a clear reason.

## Software should remove work, not relocate it

Moving a manager's spreadsheet task to a receiver, maker, or worker is not automation. A change should reduce total effort or make an unavoidable step clearer and safer.

## Operational data should emerge naturally from real work whenever possible

The most trustworthy operational data is often created when a real action occurs: receiving, scanning, completing, checking, or reconciling. Prefer that evidence over parallel reporting workflows.

## Every required action has a human cost

Each click, field, scan, interruption, and context switch has a cost. Before adding one, ask who performs it, how often, under what conditions, what it replaces, and whether the value outweighs the burden.

When a proposed feature mainly gives management more data while imposing substantial repeated entry on frontline workers, redesign it, make it optional, or decline it.
